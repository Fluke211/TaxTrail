### The pattern worth internalizing

Every warm, one-word English noun for "a place you put things" is gone. Shoebox,
glovebox, cubby, packrat, slipbox, keeper, stub — all taken, several by apps
running our exact privacy pitch. This is not bad luck; the App Store has roughly
two million apps and common nouns were exhausted years ago.

Two consequences:

1. **Stop hunting for the perfect meaningful single word.** It does not exist.
2. **A collision is a competitor signal, not a naming inconvenience** — that rule
   is now in `CLAUDE.md`, and it is what caught DocuFlexPro and Stub.

## 3. Recommendation

**Go with Slipjar. Hold TaxTrail as the backup.**

The reasoning, since it is not obvious:

**Stop asking the name to do ASO.** That is what buried us last time — chasing a
descriptive name in a saturated zone. Apple gives you two fields, and they do
different jobs:

| Field | Limit | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | 30 chars | Be ownable and memorable. Indexed for search |
| **Subtitle** | 30 chars | Carry the keywords |

So: `Slipjar: Receipt Scanner` as the name, and the subtitle does the selling.
Both of your hooks fit, and you can A/B them without a build:

- *"Tax-ready by April 15"* — the seasonal hook
- *"Never lose a receipt again"* — the everyday hook
- *"Schedule C receipts, on device"* — the differentiator

A suggestive mark like Slipjar is also the only kind you can actually defend.
"AuditProof" and "TaxTrail" are descriptive: you would be unable to stop a
competitor from using something near-identical, which is precisely the situation
we are currently digging out of.

TaxTrail is the backup because it is instantly legible and its keywords are free
money — but it is a rental, not an asset.

## 4. The ten-minute clearance procedure — Tyler runs this

Do it in this order. Stop at the first name that clears all four.

**Step 1 — App Store Connect (the authoritative test, ~2 min)**

App Store Connect → Apps → the existing **ReceiptSnap: Expense Organizer**
record → App Information → edit **Name**. Type `Slipjar: Receipt Scanner`.
ASC tells you immediately if the string is taken. If it takes, the name is
reserved to that record.

> Apple releases a reserved name if no build is submitted within 90 days of
> creating the record. The existing record already has a build history, but do
> not treat the reservation as permanent — get the production build in.

**Step 2 — USPTO (~3 min)** — [tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/),
search `slipjar`. Look for **LIVE** marks in class 9 (software) or 36 (financial
services). Dead marks do not matter. Nothing live and similar → clear.

**Step 3 — domain (~2 min)** — buy `slipjar.app`. Registrar search is the real
availability answer; my DNS check is only a hint.

**Step 4 — both stores by hand (~3 min)** — search "slipjar" in the App Store
*and* Google Play apps. Web search misses apps with no marketing site.

Tell me which name cleared and I will do the rename everywhere in one PR.

## 6. TaxTrail — full due diligence, 2026-08-10

Tyler took the shortlist to App Store Connect. Result: **bare "TaxTrail" and
"Tax Trail" were both refused**; `TaxTrail: Receipt Scanner` cleared and is now
on the app record, with subtitle *"Categorization for Schedule C"*.

Per the standing rule, a refusal is a research trigger. This is what is there.

### 6.1 What the refusal means

Nothing public holds the exact string "TaxTrail" on the App Store. App Store
Connect blocks a name if **any** developer has it on an app record — including
records that were created and never shipped. Those reservations are invisible
from outside. So the most likely explanation is a reserved, unreleased record
belonging to someone else, and there is no way to see who from here.

Practical consequence: on Apple's own namespace we are already the second
comer. Not fatal — the qualified string cleared — but it is not a clean field.

Its own description: *"expense and receipt tracking… Capture receipts using
your device's camera… everything stored locally on your device. MyTaxTrail does

advertising, or tracking. Designed for simplicity, privacy, and control."*

**That is our name root and our exact privacy pitch.** This is the ReceiptSnap
pattern again, and it has to be stated plainly rather than explained away.

What separates us, from its own feature list: it has **no OCR**. It says
*"Manually record expense details such as amount, date, and notes."* No
recognition, no categorization, no Schedule C mapping, no sales-tax handling,
no TXF or QuickBooks export. It is a camera roll with an amount field and a CSV
button. Zero ratings after seven months.

So the collision is on **name and positioning**, not on capability.

### 6.4 Domains

- **`taxtrail.com` is gone** — registered **2006-10-13**, held twenty years,
  registrar Realtime Register B.V., currently undelegated (no nameservers, so it
  serves nothing). Not listed for sale. Expiry 2026-10-13 is worth a diary note,
  but a domain held that long almost always renews.
- **`taxtrail.app` is available** (RDAP 404). This is the one to buy.
- `mytaxtrail.com` was taken 2025-12-15 by the MyTaxTrail developer.

### 6.5 Trademark

**Not cleared, and I cannot clear it.** Every route out of this environment is
blocked: `tmsearch.uspto.gov` returns 404 to the API, `uspto.report` 403s,
`trademarks.justia.com` and `web.archive.org` are blocked by the egress proxy,
and the assignment API 502s through the tunnel.

Web search surfaced **no** US mark for TAXTRAIL or TAX TRAIL. It did surface the
neighbourhood: **TAXTRACKER** (serial 85324686, class 9, mobile app software)
was filed 2011-05-19 and **abandoned** 2011-11-28 — an abandoned mark blocks
nothing. TAXSLAYER, TAXLINK, TAXCADDY and TAX SERIES are all live in the zone.

**Tyler must run this at [tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/):**
search `taxtrail`, then `tax trail`. Look for **LIVE** marks in class 9
(software), 36 (financial services) or 42 (SaaS). A live US mark for TaxTrail in
9 or 36 is a stop sign. Dead and abandoned marks are noise.

### 6.6 Google Play

**Clear.** The Play search page for `taxtrail` returns only generic tax apps —
CloudTax, TaxDome, Liberty Tax, Optima, Taxatio and similar. Nothing using the
name.

### 6.7 Verdict

**Usable. Not clean. Much better than ReceiptSnap was.**

| | ReceiptSnap (D-023) | TaxTrail |
|---|---|---|
| Exact-name apps shipping | 4 | 0 |
| Same-root apps shipping | 4 | 1 (MyTaxTrail, 0 ratings, no OCR) |
| Competitors matching our pitch **and** our capability | 3 | 0 |
| Google Play | contested | clear |
| A domain we can own | none pursued | `taxtrail.app` |

The real cost of TaxTrail is not legal, it is structural: **`Tax` + noun is the
most worked-over naming pattern in this category** — TaxTracker, TaxSlayer,
TaxLink, TaxCaddy, TaxDay, TaxAudit, TaxAct, TaxFyle. It is a name you can use
but never own, and you will always share the search results. `Slipjar` would
have been ownable. That is the trade being made, and it is a legitimate one:
legibility now versus defensibility later.

**Recommendation: proceed, with two conditions.**

1. **Run the USPTO search** (§6.5). A live US mark in class 9 or 36 stops this.
2. **Buy `taxtrail.app` before anything ships.** It also solves the
   `CONTACT_EMAIL_PLACEHOLDER` blocker in `privacy.html` / `support.html`.

MyTaxTrail is a real ASO annoyance and not much else. If it ever grows teeth,
the differentiator is already built: it cannot read a receipt.
